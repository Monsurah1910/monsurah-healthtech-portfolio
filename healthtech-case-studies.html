01. CareBridge Medical Centre: Business Discovery Phase Assessment

Healthcare operations · Business analysis · Discovery and requirements elicitation

A team-based discovery assessment exploring operational challenges at a fictional medical centre and identifying the information needed to understand its current services and business needs.

Business context and challenges

The assessment considered five connected problem areas:

  • Patient-flow inefficiencies
  • Variability in laboratory turnaround times
  • Record-management vulnerabilities
  • Fragmented referral pathways
  • Scheduling-system misalignment

My individual contribution: Requirements elicitation

Within the HealthTech Business Analysis team (Pod Polaris), my individual contribution focused on requirements elicitation. I helped develop structured discovery questions to gather relevant information from stakeholders and build an understanding of their needs, experiences and operational concerns.

The elicitation approach considered interviews, workshops and surveys as methods for engaging stakeholders and gathering information to support the discovery assessment.

How this supported the wider assessment

The elicitation work helped organise the questions that needed to be explored during discovery. It supported the team's understanding of current processes, stakeholder perspectives and areas requiring further investigation before defining potential improvements.

Contribution boundary: this was a collaborative team assessment. The contribution described above is my individual requirements-elicitation work; it should not be read as sole authorship of the full report or as evidence that recommendations were implemented.

02. Apex Health Network: Closed-Loop Medication & Pharmacy Optimisation

Five Case Model · Economic appraisal · Digital medication-management proposal

A team capstone business case examining how a large, decentralised healthcare network could strengthen medication management through a more connected, closed-loop medication and pharmacy pathway.

Business context and challenge

The project explored fragmented medication processes, manual data entry, information gaps, avoidable rework and medication discrepancies. The proposed direction considered a more integrated pathway linking prescribing, pharmacy verification, medication administration and audit information.

Proposed digital approach

The team considered an integrated solution incorporating e-prescribing, pharmacy verification, barcode and eMAR support, a real-time medication record, alerts, medication reconciliation and audit reporting. These were proposed capabilities within the case study, not a system implemented in a live healthcare service.

My individual contribution: Economic Case leadership

Within the six-member capstone team, I led the development of the Economic Case. My work included the options appraisal, examining four options and supporting the selection of Option 3 as the preferred way forward, together with the SWOT analysis to consider strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats relevant to the proposal.

The Strategic, Commercial, Financial and Management Cases were developed collaboratively by the wider team. My contribution should therefore be understood specifically as leading the Economic Case work, rather than sole authorship of the complete Five Case Model.

Wider team analysis and governance

The team applied the Five Case Model and considered clinical, pharmacy, patient, IT, leadership, finance, procurement, information-governance and supplier stakeholders. Supporting techniques included stakeholder analysis, Mendelow’s matrix, RACI, communications planning and SWOT.

Illustrative success measures

  • Reduction in medication-related errors and discrepancies
  • Improved medication-data accuracy and completeness
  • Reduced medication-processing delays and avoidable rework
  • Staff training completion and active user adoption
  • Improved patient experience and system reliability

Financial appraisal: scenario assumptions

The presentation records illustrative assumptions of £1.15 million initial investment, £190,000 net annual benefit, a 5% discount rate and an estimated five-year NPV of approximately −£327,000.

Important qualification: these are scenario figures from the training capstone, not verified supplier costs, approved budgets or realised savings. The financial model requires validation. The appraisal also highlights the importance of considering non-financial benefits, particularly medication safety and quality of care.

Key learning: a digital-health proposal needs balanced appraisal across all five dimensions. The Economic Case should compare credible options and explain why a preferred option offers the strongest overall value, rather than assuming that the most technologically ambitious solution is automatically the best choice.

05. Amberleigh Care Home: eMAR Rollout

Business Requirements Document · Revision 0.2 · 26 September 2026

This training project examined the proposed move from paper-based medication administration and record keeping to an electronic medication administration record (eMAR) process in a residential and nursing care setting.

Current-state challenges

  • Manual coordination of GP prescription changes with the community pharmacy
  • Paper controlled-drug registers and manual double sign-off
  • Manual assignment of medication rounds
  • Paper-based stock checks and discrepancy reporting
  • Paper MAR charts and physical archiving

Requirements and proposed improvements

  • Documented stakeholder needs and process flows
  • AS-IS and TO-BE process descriptions
  • Functional and non-functional requirements
  • Consideration of digital alerts, electronic sign-off, barcode-supported stock tracking and incident reporting

The BRD records a proposed deployment timeframe of Q1 2027. This is a plan in the training document, not confirmation that deployment has occurred.

06. Riverside Community Health: Remote Patient Monitoring System

Proposed digital health solution · Agile Scrum and Jira · Training scenario

Riverside Community Health is presented in the capstone scenario as a provider supporting approximately 40,000 people living with chronic conditions, including hypertension and type 2 diabetes. The existing model relies heavily on periodic clinic appointments and manually recorded home readings, limiting visibility of patients’ health between appointments.

Business need

Improve the flow of home-monitoring information to care teams, support timely review of readings and make escalation pathways more visible.

Proposed solution

A Remote Patient Monitoring system for eligible patients to submit readings through connected devices or manual entry. Physician-defined thresholds would support prioritised alerts, nurse review, clinical escalation and relevant EHR synchronisation.

Business analysis work

  • Discovery and problem definition
  • Stakeholder analysis and RACI
  • Business case, options and benefits
  • Functional and non-functional requirements
  • AS-IS / TO-BE process thinking

Agile delivery planning

  • Eight epics and 24 user stories
  • Acceptance criteria and prioritisation
  • Three-sprint delivery approach
  • Jira product backlog and Scrum board planning
Example requirement: a monitoring nurse can acknowledge a red alert so that urgent readings have visible ownership. The capstone acceptance criteria include recording acknowledgement and a target acknowledgement time within 30 minutes during operating hours.

Agile delivery approach — from backlog to sprint planning

The capstone translated the proposed Remote Patient Monitoring solution into a structured Agile backlog. The work was organised into eight epics and 24 user stories, supported by acceptance criteria, prioritisation and sprint traceability.

Product backlog — eight epics

E01 Referral, enrolment & consent
E02 Device & patient setup
E03 Reading capture & monitoring
E04 Reminders & adherence
E05 Clinical thresholds & alerts
E06 Nurse monitoring & escalation
E07 EHR integration, security & billing
E08 Reporting & management dashboard

The 24 user stories were grouped across these epics, with acceptance criteria and priorities recorded in the project tracker and Jira workspace.
