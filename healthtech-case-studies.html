01. CareBridge Medical Centre: Business Discovery Phase Assessment

Healthcare operations · Business analysis · Discovery · Requirements elicitation

An eight-page, team-based discovery assessment for a fictional medical centre. The work explored interconnected operational challenges, identified information needed to understand the current service, and framed questions for further stakeholder discovery.

Business context and challenges

The assessment examined five connected problem areas affecting the medical centre:

  • Patient-flow inefficiencies — challenges in the movement of patients through service pathways.
  • Laboratory turnaround variability — inconsistent turnaround times requiring further investigation.
  • Record-management vulnerabilities — concerns relating to the handling and management of records.
  • Fragmented referral pathways — gaps in coordination across referral processes.
  • Scheduling-system misalignment — scheduling arrangements that did not align effectively with service needs.

My individual contribution: Requirements elicitation

As a member of the HealthTech Business Analysis team (Pod Polaris), my individual contribution focused on requirements elicitation. I contributed to developing structured discovery questions designed to gather relevant information from stakeholders, understand their needs and experiences, and explore operational concerns.

The elicitation approach considered interviews, workshops and surveys as stakeholder-engagement methods. My contribution was to help shape the questions and information requirements for discovery; this should not be interpreted as sole responsibility for conducting every engagement activity or producing the full assessment report.

How the elicitation work supported the assessment

The structured questions helped the team organise what needed to be understood before considering potential improvements. They supported exploration of current processes, stakeholder perspectives, service challenges and areas where further evidence would be required.

The wider team assessment brought together the problem areas, stakeholder analysis, information requirements and a set of 25 professional discovery questions. These were collaborative project outputs, not claimed as my sole-authored deliverables.

Skills demonstrated

02. Apex Health Network: Closed-Loop Medication & Pharmacy Optimisation

Five Case Model · Economic appraisal · Digital medication-management proposal

A team capstone business case examining how a large, decentralised healthcare network could strengthen medication management through a more connected, closed-loop medication and pharmacy pathway.

Business context and challenge

The project explored fragmented medication processes, manual data entry, information gaps, avoidable rework and medication discrepancies. The proposed direction considered a more integrated pathway linking prescribing, pharmacy verification, medication administration and audit information.

Proposed digital approach

The team considered an integrated solution incorporating e-prescribing, pharmacy verification, barcode and eMAR support, a real-time medication record, alerts, medication reconciliation and audit reporting. These were proposed capabilities within the case study, not a system implemented in a live healthcare service.

Economic Case: four options considered

My options appraisal compared four approaches, setting out the advantages and disadvantages of each:

Option 0 — Do nothing

Description: maintain the current fragmented, manual medication systems. Advantage: zero upfront cost. Disadvantages: continued annual losses of over £2.1 million, with rising adverse drug event (ADE) and compliance risks.

Option 1 — Additional staffing

Description: hire additional pharmacy and nursing staff to manually cross-check medications. Advantage: fast to implement. Disadvantages: high recurring operating expenditure (OPEX), while the risk of human error remains.

Option 2 — Standalone third-party modules

Description: purchase standalone medication or inventory modules from a third-party vendor without full end-to-end integration. Advantages: faster targeted deployment and lower initial implementation complexity. Disadvantages: potential new silos, duplicated interfaces, and weaker end-to-end analytics and traceability.

Option 3 — Integrated Closed-Loop Medication Management platform

Description: procure a configurable enterprise platform and integrate it with AHN's existing EHR using a joint vendor/internal delivery team and a stage-gated Hybrid Agile approach. Advantages: mature safety-critical functionality, tailored integration, scalability across sites, and the strongest end-to-end safety, inventory and data benefits. Disadvantages: the highest change and integration effort, recurring licensing and support costs, and a need for strong clinical and technical governance.

Appraisal conclusion: Option 3 was identified as the preferred option in the case study. The appraisal recognised its broader end-to-end benefits alongside the greater implementation, cost and governance demands.

My individual contribution: Economic Case

As a member of the six-person capstone team, I led the Economic Case workstream. I developed the four-option appraisal, comparing the do-nothing approach, additional staffing, standalone third-party modules and an integrated Closed-Loop Medication Management platform. I assessed the advantages and disadvantages of each option, contributed to the recommendation of Option 3, and prepared the SWOT analysis of the preferred option.

The Strategic, Commercial, Financial and Management Cases were developed collaboratively with the wider team. This section therefore distinguishes my leadership of the Economic Case from the team's collective development of the full Five Case Model business case.

Evidence of my Economic Case contribution

The original artefacts below provide evidence of my options appraisal and SWOT analysis for the AHN capstone.

Stakeholder analysis, prioritisation and communication planning

The AHN team applied the Five Case Model to a proposed integrated enterprise platform intended to transform medication safety, pharmacy operations and clinical efficiency across 12 hospitals and 45 outpatient clinics. Within the six-member capstone team, I personally developed the AHN Communication Plan and the Power/Interest Matrix Summary using Mendelow’s Matrix. These artefacts demonstrate my individual work in stakeholder prioritisation, engagement planning, communication requirements, channels, frequency and ownership.

My individual contribution: Power/Interest Matrix Summary

I grouped stakeholders into four engagement categories:

  • Manage closely (high power, high interest): CEO, CMO, CNO, CTO, Director of Pharmacy — steering group membership, fortnightly updates and a direct escalation path.
  • Keep satisfied (high power, low/medium interest): Legal and Compliance, Data Protection Officer / Information Governance Lead, NHS England, CQC and ICO — scheduled compliance reviews, sign-off gates and minimal but timely updates.
  • Keep informed (low/medium power, high interest): floor nurses, pharmacists, IT development team, hospital/clinic site administrators, patients and commissioners — UAT involvement, training workshops, newsletters and ward briefings.
  • Monitor (low power, low interest): general low-impact external stakeholders — periodic light-touch updates and reassessment if influence or interest changes.

My individual contribution: Communication Plan

I also developed the AHN Communication Plan, mapping the information to be shared, its audience, method or channel, frequency and owner. The plan covered executive status, budget and top risks; clinical workflow, hazards and design decisions; workflow changes, prototypes, UAT and training; interfaces, defects and releases; privacy, security and controlled-drug assurance; and costs, procurement and benefits. It assigned communication responsibilities to roles including the Project Manager, Clinical Lead / Business Analyst, Business Analyst / Change Lead, IT Lead and Finance Lead, with frequencies ranging from weekly and fortnightly updates to monthly reviews and design or release gates.

Collaborative team artefacts

The stakeholder register, RACI Matrix and wider Five Case Model business case were developed collaboratively by the team. The stakeholder groups and engagement considerations were therefore part of the shared project context, while the Communication Plan and Mendelow Power/Interest Matrix Summary above are identified as my individual contributions.

Illustrative success measures

  • Reduction in medication-related errors and discrepancies
  • Improved medication-data accuracy and completeness
  • Reduced medication-processing delays and avoidable rework
  • Staff training completion and active user adoption
  • Improved patient experience and system reliability

Financial appraisal: presentation assumptions

The capstone presentation sets out an illustrative financial case based on £1.20 million base capital expenditure (CAPEX), plus a 15% contingency of £180,000, giving a total proposed funding envelope of £1.38 million.

Estimated annual gross benefits are £2.10 million, comprising £900,000 from reduced wastage and billing and £1.20 million from avoided adverse drug event (ADE) costs. After annual operating expenditure (OPEX) of £180,000, the presentation states an annual net operating benefit of £1.92 million.

  • Simple payback: 7.5 months, calculated against the £1.20 million base CAPEX.
  • Discount rate: 5%.
  • Five-year NPV: approximately £7.11 million, as reported in the presentation.
Financial interpretation and qualification: these are illustrative planning estimates from the training capstone, not verified supplier quotations, approved budgets or realised savings. The presentation recommends progression subject to procurement, clinical-safety, cybersecurity and financial validation. The reported payback uses base CAPEX; the presentation separately identifies the contingency within the total funding envelope. The NPV should be read as the presentation's five-year estimate and validated against the underlying cash-flow model, including the treatment of contingency, recurring costs and benefit timing. The estimated benefits, particularly avoided ADE costs, also require clinical and operational evidence before they could be treated as cash-releasing savings.

Key learning: a digital-health proposal needs balanced appraisal across all five dimensions. The Economic Case should compare credible options and explain why a preferred option offers the strongest overall value, rather than assuming that the most technologically ambitious solution is automatically the best choice.

03. CareSync Health: Digital Patient Experience Transformation

Digital patient experience · Business case development · Five Case Model

A facilitator-led classroom case study explored collaboratively with classmates. The exercise examined how a healthcare clinic could address revenue leakage and patient-retention challenges through a proposed digital patient-experience transformation.

Business problem and opportunity

The case scenario identified projected annual revenue leakage of $800,000, arising from two operational problem areas:

Insurance claim denials — $500,000 annually

Root causes included manual billing processes, inaccurate medical coding, delayed coverage verification and missed timely-filing windows. The scenario described rejected claims creating billing backlogs and delaying cash collection, with days in accounts receivable exceeding 45 days.

Patient churn and low retention — $300,000 annually

The scenario linked a 10% annual drop in returning patients to friction in scheduling, fragmented billing communication, limited digital access to health records and absent follow-up reminders. The stated impact was reduced patient lifetime value and additional acquisition costs.

Proposed digital transformation

The case explored a proposed Unified Patient Portal and Automated Claims Engine. The portal was intended to improve patient access and appointment interactions, while the claims engine was intended to support more accurate insurance data validation and reduce avoidable claim rejections.

Five Case Model framework

Working through the case with the facilitator and classmates provided practical exposure to structuring a business case across five connected dimensions:

1. Strategic Case

Why change was needed, the business problems, strategic fit, objectives and scope.

2. Economic Case

Options considered, SWOT analysis, cost-benefit thinking, benchmarking and the rationale for a preferred option.

3. Commercial Case

The proposed procurement approach and how the required solution and services might be obtained.

4. Financial Case

Funding requirements, capital and recurring costs, and the financial measures considered in the scenario.

5. Management Case

Governance, delivery planning, risk, compliance, communication and benefits realisation.

Learning and relevance to my BA development

This collaborative classroom exercise helped me understand how a healthcare business problem can be translated into a structured transformation proposal. It strengthened my appreciation of the relationship between strategic need, options appraisal, affordability, procurement, delivery governance and measurable benefits. The learning provided a foundation for applying business-analysis techniques in subsequent assignments and capstone projects.

Project context and attribution: CareSync was a facilitator-led classroom case study explored collaboratively with classmates. It is presented here as a learning exercise, not as an individually authored business case or a live implementation. The scenario's business figures are stated assumptions from the case study, not independently verified organisational results.
Financial interpretation: the classroom material contains financial assumptions that require reconciliation, including inconsistent currency labels and calculations that do not clearly account for recurring operating costs. For that reason, this portfolio entry focuses on the business problem and Five Case Model learning rather than presenting ROI, payback or NPV figures as validated conclusions.

06. Riverside Community Health: Remote Patient Monitoring System

Proposed digital health solution · Agile Scrum and Jira · Training scenario

Riverside Community Health is presented in the capstone scenario as a provider supporting approximately 40,000 people living with chronic conditions, including hypertension and type 2 diabetes. The existing model relies heavily on periodic clinic appointments and manually recorded home readings, limiting visibility of patients’ health between appointments.

Business need

Improve the flow of home-monitoring information to care teams, support timely review of readings and make escalation pathways more visible.

Proposed solution

A Remote Patient Monitoring system for eligible patients to submit readings through connected devices or manual entry. Physician-defined thresholds would support prioritised alerts, nurse review, clinical escalation and relevant EHR synchronisation.

Business analysis work

  • Discovery and problem definition
  • Stakeholder analysis and RACI
  • Business case, options and benefits
  • Functional and non-functional requirements
  • AS-IS / TO-BE process thinking

Agile delivery planning

  • Eight epics and 24 user stories
  • Acceptance criteria and prioritisation
  • Three-sprint delivery approach
  • Jira product backlog and Scrum board planning
Example requirement: a monitoring nurse can acknowledge a red alert so that urgent readings have visible ownership. The capstone acceptance criteria include recording acknowledgement and a target acknowledgement time within 30 minutes during operating hours.
